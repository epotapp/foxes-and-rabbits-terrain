AT THE TABLE

Rules in use

One referee. Three match modes.

Three kinds of earth

Ground

When construction starts on ground, every d6 makes a tunnel exactly one step longer: a roll of 6 draws 7 connections. The bonus depends on the starting hex, even if the tunnel crosses sand.

Sand

Every connection touching a sand hex is temporary. A connection dug in round 1 survives rounds 1, 2 and 3; it collapses when the Rabbit rolls construction in round 4. Ground-to-ground connections in the same tunnel remain. Drawing a legal tunnel over sand connections refreshes their lifetime, but the tunnel must still add at least one new connection.

Rock

Rock cannot be dug into or through. The Fox can walk across all terrain. Terrain is public information; tunnel locations and ages remain private outside the Fox’s aperture.

The Rabbit

Every turn, roll three d6 and create three straight tunnels, one per die. All three start at the Rabbit’s current hex. Use the full length, including the ground bonus, without turning or reversing. Tunnels may overlap, but each must add at least one new connection.

A tunnel is skipped only if no legal full-length straight placement exists in any of the six directions. The referee checks this automatically; a legal tunnel cannot be skipped.

Then roll one d6 and move exactly that many steps along the tunnels. Movement may turn at junctions. The starting hex costs nothing. The Rabbit’s turn is hidden from the Fox.

The Fox

The Fox must move every turn. Roll two d4, add the results, and spend the entire total. Move through adjacent hexes, with or without tunnels. Returning to the starting hex is allowed, but standing still or ending movement early is not. Capture ends the game immediately.

The viewing aperture follows the Fox and reveals information after every step.

Winning

The Fox wins immediately when it steps onto the Rabbit, even before its movement ends. A Rabbit unable to take any movement step after construction is trapped and loses. Otherwise, the Rabbit wins after surviving 25 complete rounds: 25 Rabbit turns and 25 Fox turns.

Match modes

HvH, HvA and AvA use the same rules. Choose a human or an agent for each player. A human can play either role. Two humans use a covered handoff. AvA can be watched, stepped through, or run in bulk.

Terrain conventions

  • The Rabbit moves first, with no initial tunnels.
  • Ground-to-ground tunnels persist. Sand connections expire after three rounds.
  • Rabbit movement may backtrack.
  • The Rabbit entering the Fox’s hex also causes capture.
  • Rabbit dice are private. Starting positions are public.

Mixed-terrain length and expiry follow the conventions on this page. If the Rabbit has no tunnel connection after construction, it is trapped and the Fox wins. Straight construction, overlap with a new connection, compulsory Fox movement, and skipping only impossible tunnels are confirmed rules.

The field and cover

The default field has 19 columns and 17 rows. On the standard field, FI is column 2, row 16; RI is column 12, row 6. Count from the top left. These match the marked reference. Other field sizes scale the positions proportionally.

Sight 2 reveals the Fox’s hex and two surrounding rings: up to 19 hexes. This approximates the physical aperture. Other settings are experimental variants.
